## Why Clean Language?
This blog is a bit of a double whammy combining my learning as a Mental Health advocate as well as a coach for mental health awareness week. I share how I learned about myself, by learning how to use a set of concepts to support people. The concepts themselves were literally built for this purpose: Through the act of learning the tool, you learn about yourself.  
### Source 
Caitlin Walker the originator of Systemic modelling [explains it best in the video, so give her talk a listen. ](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aVvcU5gG4KU&pp=ygUSVGVkIGNhaXRsaW4gd2Fsa2Vy )
If you like it, consider reading her book  From Contempt to Curiosity (or Audiobook in my case), which takes you through her journey and shows how powerful this approach can be.  For everything else have a look at https://cleanlearning.co.uk/

## When I work at my best
A ‘classic’ question to try the tool out is to be asked, “**When you are** working at your best, you are **like what?**” For me, the following metaphor is still vivid in my mind years on and has flowed into many more conversations and explorations.

Q: **When** you are working at your best, **you are like what**?
A: I am an octopus spinning lots of plates.

Q: **Is there anything else about** octopus?
A: It’s got lots of arms juggling the plates.

Q: **What kind of** juggling?
A: “Frantic, just about managing.”
… “Just about managing”, I didn’t know that! Where did that come from? I said it because it felt right / true, but it certainly didn’t come from the logical thinking part of my brain.
The questions were asked in a group session, and the next participant shared how when they were working at their best, it was like space filled with lots of stars. This got me interested so I asked a question myself.

Q: "Do the stars **have a colour**?"
A: "They are all different coloured flashing lights"
Like discovering the “just about managing”, I can’t write it down in logical terms, but the other participant’s metaphor felt right for my own stuff too, so next thing I know, my octopus is in space juggling lots of meteors as they zip past.
My octopus model has a lot more that I take from it, with the biggest being my joy of novelty and variety, but also that, while I *can* do 1000 things at once in bursts, it’s not sustainable and I need to manage it.
From just asking simple, curious questions about each other’s metaphors, we can draw out deep learning and understanding for both individuals, as well as group collective understanding.  

### Worked example
To unpick the concept, let’s work through an example.
It all starts with a question; **When you X, you are like what?** This could be; 
- When you are working at your best, you are like what?
- When you are safe, you are like what?
- When our team is at its best, it is like what? (Ask a group!)
Take a moment, and think about what your answer would be to the following question:
“For this blog to be as useful as possible, it would be like what?”

The wording may feel a bit awkward, but the “**it is like what?”** is very specifically crafted. Everyone thinks differently, but a decent majority of you will come up with some form of metaphor / analogy.  
To go meta: for this blog to be as useful as possible to the reader, I want it to be like a loaf of bread.
Ok… that probably doesn’t make much sense to anyone else. Crucially though, it makes sense to ME.  This might be the same for your own answer, too. For those less predisposed to thinking in analogy, you may have answered the question with “clear and to the point”. For both these answers, we now have something tangible we can discuss.
To go from these initial answers to something that helps the person understand more deeply, and possibly us along the way, we just need to show genuine interest, and ask some questions.

Q:  **What kind** of bread?
A:  A sliced loaf
Q: **Is there anything else about** ‘slices’?
A: They are useful on their own, it lets me try out if it’s tasty or not.
Q: And if it’s tasty, **then what happens?**
A: I know whether to eat more of it or not. 
Q: **Where is** the bread?
A: It’s all on the table in front of me, but ready to go in the cupboard if it’s tasty, or the bin if not.
Each of these questions enables the person to expand their model and gain a deeper understanding. Your curiosity helps draw the elements out, so they understand their stuff better, as well as helping you begin to understand it, too. 

### Depth
When tackling problems, we often try and logically think things through, but this can conflict with the “gut” feeling, or cause an [inner monkey to kick off.](https://chimpmanagement.com/the-chimp-model/) The example above is purposefully innocuous, but as you will see in my own experience below and the examples Caitlin and David Grove have shared, the real power is when the client learns something new or deeper for themselves simply from being asked to expand their metaphor in a direction they hadn't considered before.  

*“Metaphors mediate between the conscious and unconscious” – David Grove*

When we think in metaphor, it opens space for both the logical and deeper limbic (gut) parts of our brain to answer. When asked questions, some answers may be “obvious” to us already, so we just give the detail, but others…. they have the capacity to pull out things we didn’t know
## Clean language
Clean Language is based on research by David Grove, who observed how therapists speak with their clients.  The aim is to help others to expand their own understanding, without contributing to it yourself. This comes from “cleaning up” your questions as much as possible from presumption or imposition, and reiterating the clients’ own words.
*“In asking a [clean] question we do not impose upon the client any value, construct or presupposition about what they should answer”* – David Grove and Basil Panzer
When we ask questions like “**Why** didn’t you do X?” the implication is obvious, and likely to trigger a push back rather than open answer. If we instead use **“What?”** questions we are more likely to help people explore.
*Clean Language Questions – Marian Way Jan 2013*
Check out [*Clean Language Questions*](https://cleanlearning.co.uk/blog/discuss/clean-language-questions) by the experts at Clean Learning for a deeper explanation.
**Uses**
In Caitlin Walker’s Book “From Contempt to Curiosity”, and in her [**Ted Talk**](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aVvcU5gG4KU), she demonstrates how the use of clean language and systemic modelling can be used in many difficult contexts.
•  Conflict: De-escalate and enable individual reflection of how individuals feel in the moment (look into the [“Drama Triangle”](https://cleanlearning.co.uk/blog/discuss/how-to-turn-your-life-into-a-dramafree-zone-1) for more on this).
•  Diversity: Models can be a safer way of exploring and understanding each other, making it easier to speak to each other’s subconscious. From Contempt to Curiosity has some great examples of this, but it important to remember that wherever you need your clients to go, you need to be prepared to go there yourself. If you aren’t going to work on your own prejudices, then you won’t be able to support others to.
•  Collaboration: While not elaborated in this blog, modelling can be done for groups, not just individuals. Creating a shared metaphor is an incredibly powerful way to bring people together and align towards a shared interest. We are used to talking about values in a surface level way in teams and companies, but this gives space to go so much deeper.
•  Anything: Fundamentally, the concept enables us to learn about ourselves and each other, so it is applicable anywhere.  
## Me, burnout
Not long after this first experience was the start of the Russia Ukrainian war. Our training cohort included two Ukrainians, one of whom managed to give a brief goodbye as he was conscripted into the army (he’s alive, but we didn't know for a long time). Trainer John Barret ran an impromptu session to help the rest of us work through the events.  There were lots of high painful emotions for our friend, and for the world in general, but the impact was different for everyone. The metaphor that came to me was that of a recycling bin.
These events were filling up my life bucket, at a time where the work “bucket” was already overflowing. The most painful part for me being the rubbish falling into the personal life and relationships bucket next to it. There is a LOT in this metaphor for me, but the biggest thing it helped me with was getting closer to acceptance that the path I was on wasn’t sustainable.

## Wrap up
Having found so much use in clean language and metaphor, it’s something I now run sessions and workshops on myself, as well as use with family to support them through various events. My partner is about to complete her training to become a counsellor, and the amount of overlap the language and techniques have with various therapeutic models is astounding.
If you made it to the end of this blog, Congrats! I hope it has offered some insight and food for thought to talk about the big stuff in as safe a way as possible.

When I am safe you will find me in the woods, in the rain, snuggled up in a coat.
