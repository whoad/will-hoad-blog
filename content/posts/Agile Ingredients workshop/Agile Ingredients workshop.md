> [!note]+ # Background & 2024 reflections
> Back in 2018 I had a chance to run a workshop at a cross organizational conference, and one of my biggest frustrations as an agile practitioner (or human in general) was the lack analysis we typically put into the why we do things. 6 or so years later writing this note, its still a challenge!
> With that in mind I created a session that got participants to think about the component parts of there approaches from a Value, Risk, disadvantages and difficulty perspective. From there participants could bake these parts into their overall approach, and have a better reflection on what it looked like overall. 
> 
> Some elements were quite a lot to get in for an ~1 hour session, but as always giving people something tangible to work with really helped them engage. 
> While my only aim was to run one session, there was a dream of building a resource / database over time where the wider community could contribute new components, comment on others, and eventually build something to help every team think critically about how the are working together.  
> 
> ### Reflections in 2024 
> 
> As one of my first “big” sessions I ever ran back when i was a dual Dev / Scrum Master with very little professional training, I was really happy with how it went.
> 
> My career and skills have grown a lot since this workshop. I did run it a few more times internally with my company, but with the time constraints i had to run training, more focus went into either direct coaching with individuals, or focusing in on mindset to achieve the same outcome.  
> 
> ### Alternatives 
> 
> Looks back, I wasn’t the only one with this same idea. Others in the field have clearly invested heavily into building models to support this sort of analysis in different ways. 
> I haven't read into any of these with any real depth, but they mostly present themselves as full methodologies or deep dive approaches. Definitely useful and have their place, but i think there is still room for a more simplistic less directive tool to help teams understand the value and pains of there approaches. Perhaps I should give this a refreshed punt one day.

# Agile Ingredients

<!-- Column 1 -->
Simply put, agile ingredients are the component parts of our delivery practices. They are the Daily Scrum to scrum, or board and work limits to Kanban.

It’s important to think about these ingredients in isolation to ensure they are the right fit and flavour for our team and current context. This is the essence of continuous improvement, adapting and assessing your process to be as slick as possible.

Each ingredient can be summarised into a quick reference card to cover their advantages, Disadvantages, time cost, risk, and difficulty. Everyone’s version of these cards will differ, but its the act of analysis that will help develop your team.

We can group our agile ingredients with the recipes they are apart of.
