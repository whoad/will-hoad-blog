Roadmaps are just another tool. Use them without a clear intent, and they will waste your time and bite you.

Lots of chat recently on roadmaps and a great talk by Alice Hughes got me reading Mike Doherty's: ["Stop Presenting Roadmaps"](https://productleaders.substack.com/p/stop-presenting-roadmaps "https://productleaders.substack.com/p/stop-presenting-roadmaps") , which is riffing off the knowledge of Dave Snowden's recent [Posts 1](https://thecynefin.co/small-wonder/ "https://thecynefin.co/small-wonder/") [and 2](https://thecynefin.co/cbi-km-2-3-map-before-you-manage/ "https://thecynefin.co/cbi-km-2-3-map-before-you-manage/") (there is a lot more in Daves work but I found Mikes work a good read and introduction)
### Is it knowledge or guidance?

> The headline point is that you can "distinguish information from knowledge using a map and a guide.
> - A map is organised information: useful, portable, safe.
> - A guide is knowledge. They don’t consult the map; they read the terrain live, adjusts for what happened yesterday, and gets you there faster, provided you trust them."

Maps of information only work for those with shared access and understanding. Great to slice up and provide to teams with an objective, bad for large scale presentation.

Customers and Seniors need tailored guides. They don't have your context so trying to read a map is at best confusing and time consuming, they instead need something that leads them specifically to what they need. I interpret guides to be the many varied approaches we use to influence people from telling stories, targeted use cases and generally re-framing information into their context.
### Think before you write and share

> "The more uncertain and fast-moving the environment, the more the knowledge should stay tacit, held in people and conversation rather than frozen into a document. Writing it down is not free."

The point being  don't just present a roadmap and expect the world to be fixed, think about the purpose of it and how certain the information is. Most learning in the art of roadmaps over the years has been to ensure they only present certain information, and get rid of everything else.

Now, Next, Later based roadmaps are great when you know the ordering but not the dates. 

Clients will always want a "date" but the good ones understand they are not promised and managed based on variation. Make a specific guide (not a map) for them for this purpose, constantly backed by a repeated to death strategy.
### Reflections 
What I'm increasingly picking up on my project both with roadmaps and in general is the lack of visible strategy. Without the strength of direction either in a vision or a related message, then roadmaps don't have a foundation to defend themselves or hold to a direction, so you may as well skip the paperwork. 

Its just my context but the majority of road-maps are a waste of ink & life so I tend to focus on other things. Any silver bullets to help my clients gain real value from them?
