## Personal Summary

Learning log from #AgileOnTheBeach2026 Francesca Cortesi "AI is here - How will it impact product management"

**Personal takeaways**
AI is powerful, but don't panic about the FOMO fueled hype train. The lifetime of technical skills has reduced to the point where things like Prompt Engineering can be invented, proposed as a university course, and then become obsolete in less than two years. 

**The magic of humans is working in the grey area**
AI is great at visible context, but it can't solve the invisible glue of people constantly collaborating and aligning together. AI feels helpful in giving us more data and information, but I don't think it's going to make a major dent on the number of poor decision makers who "just don't get it". 

**Be intentional in learning**
Francesca gave a great practical tool to reduce the FOMO and help focus on what actually matters to you.  
Create three lists of "What I know", "What I do not know", and "What I want to know". 
Prioritising each one, and focusing on the what you actually want to know will help reduce what you don't know down. 

Francesca shared a lot more insight which I will share notes on in the comments, but rather than being a regurgitation machine I highly recommend watching her talk for yourself in a few months when published via the Agile On The Beach conference

## Notes from talk

Headline: how ill AI impact? She says she doesn't know

Where should I put my attention?
* Strategize, don't react 
* Create depth, (and judgement)
* Invest in trust

Look for the assumption. Why are we here?

Red thread. The assumption. Every company will use AI for nearly everything involved in conceiving, building and growing products.

Were expected to 
* Rethink how you work
* Stay in top of trends
* Be more efficient 
* Do more with less
* And keep running the ship
We all feel this expectation, which has some excitement and fear, fomo, uncertainty and fatigue 

Two narratives. 
* Who needs PMs? 
* PMs are the bottle necks. 
AI automates What's visible, and the job is about creating value which has never been clear or consistent 

### what is un-llm-able?
* Decide what's worth learning (in your context)
* Know what's valuable (with your depth)
* Turn context into alignment (with trust)
![[Conference Talk - AotB - AI is here - How will it impact product management -1.jpg]]
People are running as if there is a finish line to adopt, learn, change, summarise.

##### Choose intentionally
The lifespan of a technical skill is 2.5 years. Was over 5 before the pandemic. She predicts 15 months now. "Prompt engineering" a prime example.

The key skill is being good at picking what you learn and unlearn. 

###### Roadmap to curious intentionally

| What I know | What I do not know | What I want to know |
| ----------- | ------------------ | ------------------- |
|             |                    |                     |

Table of, What I know, What I do not know, What I want to know. 

Fill the table then prioritise it. Thinking about the last column, you can groom the middle one. 

Be curious and intentional without panicking. You can skip the innovation bit. 

##### Skill: Work on yourself
* Defines whats important 
* Filter the noise
* Sit uncomfortably
* Stop comparing.
In product. Clarify the focus and anchor, prioritise the insite, not the tech.

### know what is valuable 
What good looks like keeps on changing. 

Before. You -> output 100% manual 100% you
Then . Model + prompt -> magic
Now. Model+ prompt, your depth, magic. 

To be LLM augmented you need
* The model
* The prompt 
* The context 
* The training data
* Your interaction 
* Your judgement 

You have control, and need
* Clear communication 
* Market understanding 
* Industry depth
* Customer knowledge 
* Company dynamics 
LLM-augmentation = depth. 

It's all about decision makeing in the grey area.

> Vaughn Tan. "meaning makeing. Making subjective decisions about relative value of things."

Value = subjective decisions of worth.

Subjective to individuals and groups. 
Some people call it taste, but it's subjective decision. (She works in fashion)

##### Skill: Know what is valuable.
* Change assumptions
* Divide objective from subjective decisions 
* Form your opinion
In product, domain, customer and business depth. 

### The Human touch
Context is not alignment 
![[Conference Talk - AotB - AI is here - How will it impact product management - 2.jpg]]
Who bridges the context to alignment 

Build the bridge, from convicted to co-creating 

* Listen to understand 
* Be open to be challenges
* Spot the unsaid 
* Spot where silos meet
AI is pushing us towards fast augmentation, but we need the humans for alignment. (And value)

##### Skill: Connect with humans
* Understand the nuances and unsaid
* Excel at incentive mapping 
* Build trust 
For product. From convincing to co-create. If you convince it will make a really shallow product, need to be open to take in what is missing.
Time to value > time to market.

You can go fast, or you can intentionally go in the right direction.
