No bulkhead made this so much easier! without taking the seat off there is just (only just) enough space to change the main battery 

Fitting the Leisure battery was the first time I have done anything on a vehicle that wouldn't just be considered “home décor”. 

The battery I ordered while the right width and length, was mm to tall for the seat to move. ended up shaving down the plastic feet at the bottom of the battery bucket to ensure it fit. 

For electrics, i never wanted anything super fancy, or to power anything heavy duty like fridges. 
However during my research it was clear that my 2014 Transit custom had a smart alternator, and in turn meant I couldn't get away with the cheap battery to battery solution. 
With that in mind, I opted for a [CTEK D250SE kit](https://simplysplitcharge.co.uk/2mtr-ctek-d250se-12v-20amp-dual-dc-dc-battery-to-battery-charger-kit-with-70amp-10mm-cable.html) from [https://simplysplitcharge.co.uk/](https://simplysplitcharge.co.uk/). 
There are a LOT of guides online about van electrics so I wont try and imitate them here. It wound up being a lot simpiler than they make it out to be though, so just go for it.
It did help that my kit came with a wiring diagram, and the fact that the battery was already where i needed it rather than training things from the bonnet. 

Here is where the “hobby project” kicked in. My new fancy battery charger can also manage incoming solar power….. it would be rude not to install one too!
I found a reasonably priced (£110 I think) for a 110W solid panel. With the CTEK i didn't need to get any additional kit, was was as simple as glue it on, wire the cable and sorted

I will likely have made the pros mad, but it is attached with pure Sikaflex. By the time I realised that I probably should have bolted it I had already insulated and panelled everything. 4 years in with the occasional check its still rock solid. For peace of mind I would bolt it next time.
